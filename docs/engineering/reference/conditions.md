**Cloud inversion v2: the warm layer measured from the valley floor** (`docs/engineering/inversion-v2-plan.md`, Phase 1b; V202; live on merge, no shadow period): `InversionScoreCalculator` compares 925 hPa with the viewpoint cell's own 2 m temperature, which on a fell top is the fell, not the valley the cloud sits in. For a site with a confirmed valley floor (`InversionProfileEligibility`: `elevation_m` >= 200, `overlooks_water`, and `valley_floor_m/lat/lng` all set) `InversionScoreCalculatorV2` (pure, `com.gregochr.goldenhour.service`) walks the temperature profile UP from the floor. The profile is a separate Open-Meteo request at the floor's lat/lng WITH `elevation=<valley_floor_m>` (`OpenMeteoClient.INVERSION_PROFILE_PARAMS`, 22 hourly variables (Phase 2 added mid and high cloud, Phase 3 the wind speed at the five pressure levels): valley 2 m temperature, dew point, low, mid and high cloud, visibility, 10 m wind, and temperature, geopotential height and wind speed (m/s) at 975/950/925/900/850 hPa; never added to `FORECAST_PARAMS`, so no mist input moved). Per hour: start at (h0, T0) = (the floor, the valley cell's 2 m temperature), take the levels at least 50 m above h0 (the summit trap), skip null levels (a profile with one valid level still scores; the 2c fixture has no 900 hPa), and take every maximal strictly rising run along the points (the floor point, then the valid levels; equal temperatures are not a rise). A run's strength is its top minus its base, rounded to 0.1 C; a run weaker than `MIN_LAYER_STRENGTH_C` = 0.3 C is noise and is discarded, and the hour's layer is its STRONGEST remaining run (a tie keeps the lower one), so a small surface blip cannot mask a deeper layer above it and a 0.04 C wobble is no layer. **Surface-based** = the run starts at the floor and its top is the FIRST valid level (a shallow radiation-fog layer, base h0); anything else is **elevated**: a run that starts at the floor and goes on past the first level has base = the FIRST valid level (the floor point is never the base of an elevated layer), and any other run has base at its own start. Known discontinuity, deliberately conservative for the first winter: a viewpoint between h0 + 100 m and the first level can flip between edge (surface-based) and inside (elevated) on a small change at the 950 hPa level. Hours examined are anchored to the event SLOT (sunrise: the slot and the four hours before; sunset: the slot and the two after, as mist does); the strongest layer wins, a tie goes to the hour nearest the slot. **Clearance** (E = the viewpoint's stored elevation): elevated, E below the base = inside (cap 6), E from the base up to the next level above it = the edge (cap 8), E at or above that level = clear; surface-based needs fog evidence in the same window (valley visibility under 5 km at any hour, or a gap of at most 1 C with wind of at most 2 m/s in the SAME hour) and then E at least h0 + 100 is the edge (cap 8), otherwise cap 6; the viewpoint's own cell at the event slot with low cloud >= 80% AND visibility < 5 km is in cloud (cap 6). **Surface terms** come from the valley cell at the event slot, whichever hour supplied the layer: moisture `MoistureScoring.gapScore` (called, not changed), wind 0-3 (the old steps) and sea evidence 0-2 (2 for valley low cloud >= 50% at the event or event-window visibility under 1 km, 1 for under 5 km). A layer of at least 2.0 C allows 10, a weaker one caps at 8, no layer caps at 6. Every term and cap is whole, so the score is whole (a test pins it) and the prompt's rounding and a reader's raw `>= 9` cannot disagree. The thresholds are priors from four mornings; the known fragile ones (visibility 4,960 vs 5,000 m at Thirlmere, Cat Bells 451 m against the 975 hPa height of 432-471 m, Blencathra against 925 hPa at 861-910 m) are listed in the calculator's Javadoc. ⚠️ **Design consequence, intended: no roster site below 600 m can exceed 8 under v2.** An elevated "clear" needs E at or above the level above the base (never below about 640 m), so the STRONG band and the "Valley inversions" topic come only from summits; a low site cannot see a deep sea. **Wiring:** `ForecastService.fetchWeatherAndTriage` attaches the profile (sliced to the event slot and its night side) to `AtmosphericData` as ONE appended nullable `InversionV2Result`, in its own try/catch outside the weather fetch, then `ForecastDataAugmentor.augmentWithInversionScore` computes BOTH scores and stores one. A site without a confirmed floor, a failed fetch, the switch off or a profile with no usable hours keeps the old score exactly as before; a profile failure never fails a slot or leaves mist unscored. **Stored value:** v2 null with a profile = the old score; the old score null but v2 scoring = v2 capped at 6; otherwise v2's value, except that **below 600 m (`RAISE_CAP_MAX_ELEVATION_M`, `photocast.inversion.v2.raise-cap-enabled`, default true) v2 may raise a site by at most one point over the old score** (owner decision 2026-10-30, option A: false positives such as the 15 Oct Cat Bells case cost a prompt block and a rating boost; summits are exempt because the old score reads their own clear cell and would hold confirmed inversions down; with whole scores this also means a below-MODERATE site cannot be lifted to STRONG). V2 may lower a score freely. One INFO line per scored slot: `[INVERSION v2] <site> <date> <event> old=<n> new=<n> regime=<...> base=<m> top=<m> strength=<C> viewpoint=<m> valley=<m> caps=<...> night=<%|-> optionB=<bool> wind=<m/s|->@<hPa|->` (`new` is the stored value, `caps` lists every cap that applied; `night` is the clear-night measure, `-` with no night data; `wind` is the summit wind of Phase 3, `-@-` for a surface-based or no-layer slot or with no reading). **The prompt** (user message only; the system prompt, its "~760m" wording and the batch cache prefix are untouched) gains a standalone `Warm layer: +Z C from about X m to Y m above a valley at h0 m; this viewpoint (E m) is clear of it / at its edge.` line in place of the "Measured reversal" line when v2 drove the stored score; the fallback path is byte-identical. **Storage and the series split (V202):** `slot_atmosphere.inversion_score_v1` (the old calculator's score) and `inversion_score_v2_raw` (v2 before the raise cap) are written on EVERY write and are NULL when v2 did not compute (a cleared floor, a failed fetch, the switch off), so a stale value cannot survive; `inversion_score` keeps the value the readers use. **The `INVERSION_CALC` series therefore switches over per SITE, not at one date**: the EXACT v2-era marker is `inversion_score_v2_raw IS NOT NULL`; a row with `inversion_score_v1 IS NOT NULL` is a v2-era row in all but the rare case below (the exact marker is `inversion_score_v2_raw IS NOT NULL`; the two differ only on the rare slot where the old calculator could not score), and the series must be read in those two halves per site. **Switch and revert:** `photocast.inversion.v2.enabled` (default true) is read at startup, so reverting is an environment variable plus a backend restart, which also clears the briefing and hot-topic memos; off, every site is scored exactly as before and no profile is fetched. Clearing a site's valley floor in the location editor puts that one site back. **Operational:** the owner sets valley floors through the Phase 1-pre editor (Suggest, check the model cell, save); a site without one changes nothing. The fixtures in `src/test/resources/inversion-v2/` pin historical cells fetched WITHOUT `elevation=`; production's cell differs slightly. **The clear night (Phase 2, sunrise only, live on merge):** a clear night lets valley air cool and a sea form; a cloudy one holds it back. `InversionProfile.sliceForEvent` cuts a separate `nightHours` list (21:00 Europe/London the evening before to 05:00 local, inclusive, ended at the slot when the slot is earlier than 05:00; 10 hours on the October fall-back night, 8 in March; empty for SUNSET and the whole-forecast profile) so `hours()` (the walk, fog evidence, minimum visibility) never sees the night. Per night hour the measure is max(mid, 0.5 x high, lid), the lid being that hour's valley low cloud when its valley visibility is at least 5 km and 0 otherwise (a low deck over good visibility stops the cooling; low cloud in poor visibility is the fog or sea itself, so valley fog forming overnight does not count against the night). Only hours with both mid and high non-null count, the mean is of the unrounded values, and fewer than 6 counted hours or none before local midnight is "no night data" (no cap, no Option B). **Caps by regime** (a night cap only ever lowers a score, and enters `caps` only when it binds): surface-based, the full measure at or over 70 caps at 6 (`overcast-night(6)`), at or over 40 at 8 (`cloudy-night(8)`, which in practice never binds because a surface-based layer is already at most 8); elevated, only the night's mean MID cloud, at or over 70 caps at 8 (`night-mid(8)`: an elevated sea survives cirrus, frontal altostratus is what threatens it); no layer, nothing (already 6). **Option B** (owner decision): a clear, calm, moist night (night measure under 40, valley wind of at most 3 m/s in at least half the night hours that have a wind reading, and a valley gap of at most 1 C in at least one night hour) counts as fog evidence for the SURFACE-BASED regime only, so an unconfirmed viewpoint at least h0 + 100 m up becomes the edge (cap 8, recorded `night-fog(8)` in place of `edge(8)`) instead of cap 6. It does not touch the sea term and never lifts the weak, in-cloud, night or raise caps. Cap order in `caps`: clearance, weak, in-cloud, night, wind, then the augmentor's no-v1 and raise. The prompt's `Warm layer:` line gains `; under a cloudy night` when the night cap bound, or `; after a clear, calm night` when Option B made the edge; otherwise it is byte-identical. All thresholds are named constants in `InversionScoreCalculatorV2` and priors from a handful of nights (Keswick 4 Nov 47.3, Glenridding 15 Jan 34.8, Scales 15 Oct 0.6). The historical-forecast fixtures have mid cloud at 0 throughout (a model artefact), so their night figures are effectively high cloud only. `InversionV2Result` carries night %, night mid %, `optionB`, `nightCapBound` and `optionBBound`. **Wind at summit height (Phase 3, live on merge):** wind applies to the ELEVATED regime only (the surface-based regime already has the 10 m valley wind term, and a low-level jet over a surface inversion is the classic fog set-up; no layer is already 6). At the EVENT SLOT hour, take the larger of the wind at the level at the chosen layer's base and at the next valid level above it (the churn that erodes a sea is at the cloud top, in the layer's lower part, and wind above a strong inversion does not mix down). The layer may have been chosen from another hour of the window, so the layer's base HEIGHT is matched to the nearest valid level of the SLOT hour's profile (a tie goes to the higher level); a level is valid when its height is at least h0 + 50 m (the walk's filter), a level with no wind is skipped, and with no wind at either level there is no wind cap. **Caps** (priors, named constants `SUMMIT_WIND_MARGINAL_MS` = 12 and `SUMMIT_WIND_VETO_MS` = 18): a wind of at least 12 m/s caps at 8, at least 18 m/s caps at 6, recorded in `caps` as `summit-wind(8)` or `summit-wind(6)` only when it binds. The confirmed Helvellyn morning (15 Jan 2025) had 10 to 12 m/s at 925 hPa from 05:00 to 08:00, and we have no observed case of wind destroying a sea. The 10 m valley wind term is unchanged. The prompt's `Warm layer:` line gains `; wind across the top about N m/s at the X hPa level` after any night clause when the wind cap bound (only the cap of 8 can show, since the block needs a stored 7 or more); otherwise it is byte-identical. `InversionV2Result` also carries `windMs`, `windLevelHpa` and `windCapBound`. The fixture rows move not at all (every chosen wind is under 12 m/s).
