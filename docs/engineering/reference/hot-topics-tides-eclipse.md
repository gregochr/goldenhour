# Tide runs, lunar eclipse, tide data, storm surge

> Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-22 to keep the always-loaded file small.
> Read the section you need; this file is not loaded automatically.

**Tide: what is free and what is Pro (tide Pro gate, `docs/engineering/tide-pro-gate-plan.md`)**: free for every role are the tide state and direction at the light, the nearest extreme, the range and its anomaly, the hot topics themselves (spring, king, big seas, surge: the two-question rule) and Claude's prose. **Since tide-light B1 (2026-10-26) a spring or king topic's alignment is Pro and Admin only:** `HotTopic.forLite()` (applied by `BriefingRoleShaper` at the HTTP edge, never to the cached or live service object) serves a non-Pro reader `detail` = `liteDetail` (the "N coastal location(s)" clause the two strategies build with no alignment text and attach via `HotTopic.withLiteDetail`; an `@JsonIgnore` in-memory carrier, so it is null after a restart's JSON reload or on a simulated topic and `detail` is then null: fail closed) and no `tideRun`, `eventType`, `eventTime` or `tideMetrics.sunriseAlignedCount`/`sunsetAlignedCount`; the topic, its label, date, regions, facts and size stay (the two-question rule). Window badges take the same sentence and lose `eventTime`; big seas lose `alignedEvent`/`gapMinutes`/`gapSide`/`structures[].aligned`. Every `HotTopic` wither must forward `liteDetail`. Pro and Admin get the window's tide curve (`BriefingWindowTide.curve`, positions, `extremes`, `heightAtWindow`) and the per-location match (`LocationTideFact.tideAligned`, `tideAlignmentQuality`, `tideShortfall`, `tideFitPhrase`, `tideHeight`, `tideLevel`, and the slot flags "Tide aligned" / "Tide not aligned"). `GET /api/briefing` strips the Pro fields for LITE at the controller (`BriefingRoleShaper`); `GET /api/tides*` is Pro and Admin only (403 for LITE, 2026-10-26) and `GET /api/forecast/{id}` nulls the tide heights and match for LITE; the `TIDE_MISALIGNED` stand-down verdict stays free.

**Hot topics on the served briefing are recomputed per assembly, and the assembled briefing is memoised up to 30 s** (`BriefingService.getCachedBriefingForApi`, performance plan P1b). A simulation toggle (`HotTopicSimulationService.setEnabled` / `setTypeActive`, which also drives the lunar-eclipse simulation) and an aurora state write bump `ServedBriefingEpoch`, so it shows on the very next request; nothing here is "live on every serve" any more. See `architecture-decisions.md` ("Hot topics are recomputed per serve") for the full key and the list of bump sites.

**Hot-topic window anchors**: ⚠️ a non-day-scoped hot topic needs an event, a fixed policy in `HotTopicEventEnricher.EVENT_BY_TYPE` or one its detector stamps (`EVENT_STAMPED_BY_DETECTOR`: MIST, STORM_SURGE and the two eclipses, per (date, event) topics whose clock time the enricher completes), or `PlanWindowProjector.keysFor` drops it from every Plan card without an error (storm surge was dropped that way from 2026-08-29 to 2026-10-22); `HotTopicWindowAnchorRosterTest` enforces it over `TopicRarity.rankedTypes()`, with `CLEARANCE` (no live strategy) the one named exception. A surge topic's figures are sampled at the next high water after its event.

**Tide runs (Hot Topics)**: ⚠️ **`HotTopic.tideRun` lost its renderer but is NOT write-only** (it was, between the Coming up redesign's P6 and v2.19.9). `KingTideHotTopicStrategy.alignmentInfo` reads `alignedEvent` to build the pill's headline, and `HotTopicEventEnricher.resolveTideEvent` reads it to set `eventType` — which is what decides, per window, whether `PlanWindowProjector.badgeFor` keeps the alignment sentence or substitutes that window's own water. So the run row steers a live, user-visible branch on both Plan cards of every tidal day, without being drawn anywhere. Its only *chart* renderer, `TideRunRow.jsx` (the 24-hour cosine-interpolated curve against the solar events — night shaded, amber solar rules — with the pill's `SPRING RUN n/N` chip), was deleted in the Coming up redesign's P6 (`docs/engineering/coming-up-plan.md` D7) along with the `HotTopicStrip` panel that mounted it; the Coming up tab's own tide-run coverage (`ComingUpTideSparkline`, a fixed-phase cosine unrelated to this field) is a different code path with a different chart, not a replacement reader of this field. The backend derivation below stays — an owner call, the same treatment v1 retirement D2 gave other now-readerless fields — and is recorded here so its removal reads as a decision, not an accident. A spring/king tide is a multi-day **run**, not n unrelated days | `TideRunBuilder` → `TideRunDay` (nullable `HotTopic.tideRun`, no migration — rides the `daily_briefing_cache` JSON) derives per day: range vs the location's own mean, every extreme in the **Europe/London** local day, that day's sunrise/sunset, sea state, and a plain-language alignment verdict | One representative coastal location for the **whole run** (biggest single-day range), named in the footer as the caveat | Chart **replaces** the fact chips; days with no derivable range fall back to them | **The pill's headline is derived from the same run row the chart draws** — `KingTideHotTopicStrategy.alignmentInfo(day, nonExpired, unaligned, passed)` reads `TideRunDay.alignedEvent`, and a null run row yields *silence* rather than a denial. Two rules keep the two lines from arguing, and both were adversarial-review findings against the first cut: `alignedEvent` follows **the water the verdict names**, not `usefulPoint` (verdict rules 2 and 3 name the *other* extremum when the useful water misses the light and that one does not — keying off `usefulPoint` alone printed "no alignment" above `HW 06:18 · 58m after sunrise`), and an alignment with an **already-passed** event gets its own wording (`KING_ALIGNMENT_PASSED` / `SPRING_ALIGNMENT_PASSED`) rather than collapsing onto the denial, because the chart, the verdict and the aligned-day editorial line are all still on that card. `aligned` and `phrase` stay keyed to the useful water — they mark what a reader of *that* run type came for. | **The headline names its own scope, from a roster-wide tally computed off the same geometry** — `TideRunBuilder.rosterAlignment` measures every coastal location at *its own* sunrise/sunset with the same rule the representative's row uses, carried as `TideRunDay.RosterAlignment(sunriseAligned, sunsetAligned, measured)`, so the pill reads `tide aligned with sunrise at 47 of 61 coastal locations` rather than pairing one coastline's geometry with the roster's size. Identical rules are load-bearing: they make the representative a member of its own tally, so an aligned chart can never print above a zero. Costs **no new queries** — `fetchExtremes` already holds every location's extremes; only the per-location solar times are new. ⚠️ **Do not "fix" `forecast_evaluation.tide_aligned` and go back to counting it.** That was considered and rejected twice: it is written only by the *synchronous* engine the batch consolidation is retiring, and it answers a **preference-weighted** question (did the tide *state* match each location's configured `TideType` — production has 42 of 61 accepting `HIGH`) where the badge asks an astronomical one true at all 61. The `countTideAlignedByTargetType` query and both strategies' `ForecastEvaluationRepository` dependency are gone; the drill-down's `TideMetrics` is fed from the same tally | **The denominator is the coastal roster, not the measured subset** — they diverge when a location has no stored extremes, and "of 52" on an aligned card beside "· 61 coastal locations" on the unaligned card of the same run is two totals for one roster. An unmeasured location counts as not aligned: it understates the fraction, which is the safe direction | **`HotTopicEventEnricher.resolveTideEvent` treats the run row as authoritative** and takes `SolarEventFreshness`. Both rules were review findings against the first cut: falling through to the tally when `alignedEvent` is null printed a lead and a window badge above the headline's own denial (harmless only while the tally came from an empty column), and the tally is **clock-free**, so without the freshness check a card saying "tide alignment already passed" still pointed a lead at that morning. A null `eventType` makes `PlanWindowProjector` drop a **window-scoped** topic silently — no badge, no day — which is how all of this surfaced. ⚠️ **It no longer drops a TIDE.** Since v2.19.9 `keysFor` tests `isDayScoped` *before* the `eventType` guard and buckets `KING_TIDE`/`SPRING_TIDE` onto **both** of the date's windows: a spring or king tide is a claim about the day, and a null `eventType` there means only that the alignment has passed or that no water reached the light — neither of which stops the day being tidal. The alignment sentence stays on the aligned window; the other window states its own nearest water, **read from `BriefingWindowTide`** (never re-derived from the run row, whose extremes are clipped to the civil day and whose geometry belongs to a separately-selected representative — both were shipped as bugs in v2.19.9 and reverted) It was a `forecast_evaluation.tide_aligned` count, which asks a different question (did the tide *state* match each location's own `TideType`, in a golden/blue-hour-sized window) and cannot distinguish "nobody aligned" from "no rows written" — so on a king tide, where the useful water is HIGH, every LOW-preference location counted for nothing and the card read "no tide alignments" above a chart saying `HW 04:58 · 39m before sunrise`. The counts still drive the **drill-down** (`TideMetrics` sunrise/sunset counts), which is the question they actually measure | `TideRunDay.highWaterRank` (distance to the highest water on record) and `highWaterAnomaly` (excess over the spring threshold) were removed in the dead-code sweep phase D: nothing read them, and `TideRunDay` now also drops `bestAligned` and `alignmentPhrase`

**Lunar eclipse** (`docs/engineering/lunar-eclipse-plan.md`, L0–L7): a `LUNAR_ECLIPSE` hot topic, an
`ALMANAC` `lunar-eclipse` entry and a per-location "dawn race" — the moon racing the brightening or
darkening sky to the horizon — extending the solar `ECLIPSE` topic's slots rather than reworking
them. `LunarEclipseCatalog` is a static, hand-seeded table of NASA Besselian-style contacts (`P1 U1
U2? max U3? U4 P4`, UTC, every umbral eclipse March 2025–2030 visible somewhere in the British Isles
— penumbral eclipses are deliberately not seeded, since a row that can never raise a topic is a row a
future session un-suppresses); `LunarEclipseCalculator.sight(eclipse, lat, lon)` derives each
location's own altitude/bearing at maximum and its set/rise-in-shadow answer from `solar-utils`'
`LunarCalculator`/`MoonriseMoonsetCalculator`, memoised per date (eligibility: ≥3° at max, **or** ≥3°
for a contiguous 30 min somewhere in `[u1, u4]`). ⚠️ **Two types share one channel, not one type with
a `body` field** — this codebase has no `HotTopicType` enum and no shared style catalogue to switch
on, so `LUNAR_ECLIPSE` is its own hot-topic type (rarity rank 2, after solar `ECLIPSE`) that maps
onto the *existing* eclipse channel (colour, glyph family, filter chip) on the client, rather than
threading a `body` discriminator through `HotTopic`/`Badge`/`AlmanacEvent`/`ComingUpEntry` and every
switch keyed on type. `BriefingSlot.eclipse` (nullable `EclipseSight`, no migration — rides
`daily_briefing_cache` JSON like `TideInfo`) carries the per-location, per-window sight: altitude,
bearing, moonset/moonrise, `setsInShadow`/`risesInShadow`, and (only on a window close enough to
twilight) a `race` (`DAWN`/`DUSK`/null) with four served light `stops` for the track's gradient — a
high, leisurely eclipse has a sight with no race, and the popup then shows facts only. The admin hot-
topic simulation template attaches this SAME fixed sight to every slot of its simulated window at
**serve time only** — it is never persisted, which is the one thing that makes the feature
browser-verifiable before the next live eclipse actually occurs. `DawnRace` (`components/DawnRace.jsx`
+ pure geometry in `utils/dawnRace.js`) draws the track in the popup, `aria-hidden`, with one visible
accessible sentence stating the whole answer (the tide-run chart's own rule) — `raceModel`/
`raceSentence`/`eclipseSpotLine` are filter/map/select over already-served instants, not a
derivation (the `comingUpSparkline.js`/`dawnRace.js` precedent the Backend-heavy bullet names below).
The Plan card's chip gets a clocked emphasised pill (`CLOCKED_TOPIC_TYPES`, shared with solar
`ECLIPSE`) reading the eclipse's own maximum time after a divider. ⚠️ **No `clearToDeg` anywhere** —
the solar handoff's "clear to the west N of M" and its west-horizon ranking were dropped for want of
terrain data, and the lunar work does not fake one: the honest per-spot substitute is the served
altitude/bearing/set-or-rise-in-shadow line, mounted as `EclipseSpotLine`
(`components/map/EclipseSpotLine.jsx`) — a sibling block right after `TideFitBlock` in both
`LocationFourDaySheet` and `MapCallout` (L7), fed by `utils/locationSheet.buildEclipseIndex` via the
same `lookupForWindow` join `TideInfo` already uses. **No promoted strip and no T−1 promotion rule**
(§6 Q1's default) — the promoted-strip machinery the solar handoff assumed was retired at plan-matrix
M5 before this feature existed, so the evening-before need is carried by the clocked chip and the
popup alone; a page-level strip is an open owner decision, not a gap in this feature. Coming up
carries a 🌘 glyph, a `next`-from-the-UK accent row and a dashed-top `aside` exposure note ("No
filter needed — bracket, the shadow is ~10 stops under the lit edge"), never `safetyNote` (the design
is explicit the lunar note carries no safety warning, unlike the sun's). The tab badge's rarity gap
(`lunar-eclipse-mean-gap-days: 900.0`) is a documented long-run estimate, not the seeded catalogue's
own mean (~290 days over six gaps, March 2025–2030 — that window is unusually dense); lowering it to
match would be a re-census decision, recorded in-line beside the config value.

**Tide point and fetch provenance** (tide-source-integrity-plan P1a): `locations.tide_lat` / `tide_lng` (both or neither) are an optional point sent to WorldTides in place of the pin, for a location whose own sea area is served by a model cell that gives wrong times. `LocationEntity.getEffectiveTidePoint()` is the tide point if set, else the pin; `WorldTidesIngestionService` sends that, and only that, on the forward and backfill paths. It applies to WorldTides only: Open-Meteo marine, solar times, the observer window, surge and weather keep the pin. V206 sets ten points as a snapshot of a neighbour's coordinates (they do not follow a later move of the neighbour). Every WorldTides call writes a `tide_fetch` row (reason code, request point, `atlas`, `station`, response point, datum, credits, extremes stored; `success = false` rows for failures). Rows are written by `TideFetchRecorder` in a `REQUIRES_NEW` transaction of their own, best effort: a failing provenance INSERT is logged and can neither fail nor roll back the fetch's extremes. When the fetch runs in a transaction (`TideService`'s `@Transactional`), a success row is written in `afterCommit` (so it exists only if the extremes committed) and a failure row in `afterCompletion`, whatever the outcome; with no transaction it is written at once. `TideService` is built with `new`, so the recorder is a Spring bean passed in to its constructor. **Point-change re-seed**: `resolveFetchWindow` first compares the effective point (tolerance 1e-6 degrees) with the request point of the location's latest successful *forward* row (`SEED`/`RESEED`/`TAIL`/`POINT_CHANGE`, never `BACKFILL`); a difference re-seeds the whole forward window with reason `POINT_CHANGE`, ahead of every other branch. A location with no forward row is compared with its pin, so unchanged locations do not re-seed on first deploy. `GET /api/locations` carries `tideLat` / `tideLng`. **Admin edit** (P1b): `PUT /api/locations/{id}` takes `tideLat` / `tideLng` / `clearTidePoint`, validated like the valley-floor triple (both or neither, clear rejected alongside values, in range) plus a 25 km limit from the pin (a tide point is a neighbour a few km away; a larger distance is a typo or the wrong coast). `tide_lat` / `tide_lng` are plain columns (not `updatable = false`), so `LocationService.update` writes them with the rest of the entity, as it does the valley-floor fields. When a coastal location's effective point changes, `LocationService` registers an after-commit callback that calls `TideService.fetchAndStoreTideExtremesInNewTransaction` (a `POINT_CHANGE` re-seed, about 14 credits); `REQUIRES_NEW` is needed because a plain `@Transactional` call inside `afterCommit` would join the finished transaction and never commit. A failure is logged and never fails or rolls back the edit; the weekly refresh retries. Any pin move on a coastal location also re-reads tides straight after the save (the effective point moves with the pin, so it costs WorldTides credits), and a pin move that leaves a stored tide point more than 25 km away is a 400. Coordinates must be finite (NaN and infinities are rejected, for the valley floor too). The admin form (`LocationManagementView`, coastal locations only) shows the pair with a Clear button and sends the tide keys only when they differ from what the server holds. **Diff-on-write and source-change alerts** (P2, V207): the tail fetch starts `TAIL_OVERLAP_DAYS` = 7 days before the frontier (still clamped to `startOfDay`, so it never reaches stored history), so every weekly fetch re-buys one stored week (about +1 credit per location per week; a tail now bills three credits). Before the delete, `WorldTidesIngestionService` loads the stored extremes in the window, pairs each with the nearest unused new extreme of the same type within ±3 h (`TideDiff.compute`) and records `overlap_pairs`, `max_lag_delta_min`, `max_height_delta_m` on the success `tide_fetch` row. A **change** is more than 5 min or more than 0.05 m on any pair. `SEED` and `POINT_CHANGE` skip the diff (nothing stored / a difference is expected); `RESEED` compares its whole window for free. The new values are stored as before. A failing diff read is logged and the merge proceeds with NULL diff columns. A fetch is reported (as a `TideSourceChange`, returned by `fetchAndStoreTideExtremes`) when it showed a change, or when its atlas, station or response point differs from the previous successful forward row **at the same request point** (a previous row that recorded no source is not compared). Alert state is the provenance log itself: a difference is reported only if the previous successful forward row did not already show one (`TideFetchEntity.showedChange()`), so it is not repeated until a fetch has shown none; no extra table or in-memory latch to lose on restart. `ScheduledForecastService.doRefreshTideExtremes` (the one body behind the `tide_refresh` schedule, Run Now and `POST /api/forecast/run/tide`) collects the returned changes and sends one `AdminAlertService.sendTideSourceChangeAlert` email after the last location, with before/after examples, saying the fix is a tide point; a failed send is logged and never fails the refresh. Single-location fetches from `LocationService` (add/edit) are not alerted.

**Marine (sea state)**: two carriers, two grains, both written by `MarineWaveRefreshService` in the briefing cycle from batched Open-Meteo Marine calls, 20 exact coordinates per request (`MarineClient.fetchMarineBatch`; no grid-cell dedupe; a lone coordinate goes through the single-point exchange because Open-Meteo answers one point with a bare object; a failed chunk persists nothing for its locations but their high-water refresh still runs and clears stale rows; existing `marine_wave` rows are read in one query and saved with one `saveAll`; high waters come from the build's `TideExtremeSnapshot` when `BriefingService` passes one, else the live query) (`MarineClient`, hourly `wave_height`, `swell_wave_height`, `wave_direction`, `wave_period`, `swell_wave_period`) | `marine_wave` (V123) is **event grain**: one row per (location, date, SUNRISE|SUNSET), read by the king, spring and storm-surge pills, `StormSurgeFactsBuilder`, `TideRunBuilder` and the window tide roll-up (V123's "aligned high-water time" wording is wrong; see V188's header) | `marine_high_water` (V188) is **high-water grain**: for locations with a `coastal_structure`, one row per daylight high water (the +-90 minute window around the `tide_extreme` high water, clipped to civil dawn..dusk of its UK civil date via `ForecastHorizon` and `SolarService`) holding the peak-hour wave, period, swell and direction. The structure columns are migration-written only (no admin UI); local H2 gets them from `BigSeasLocalFixtureSeeder` (`photocast.big-seas.seed-local-fixture=true`, `src/local`).

**Coming up tide-run shoulder days (tide-source-integrity-plan.md P3):** a run whose edge narrowly misses the spring bar shows that day beside the run as an outlined bar. `TideSizeIndex.Sizes.bestRatio` is the roster-best `1.25 x dayMaxHigh / springThreshold` per date (from the same rounded threshold `springOn` tests, so a ratio above 1.25 is exactly a spring day; locations with no spring threshold are skipped), and `TideAlmanacSource` records a non-qualifying day immediately before or after a run as a shoulder when its ratio is at least `coming-up.scoring.shoulder.min-ratio` (1.20), up to `max-per-side` (1) a side. The measured window is padded by `MAX_RUN_WALK_DAYS + 1`. The dates ride the event meta as `shoulderDates` (comma-separated ISO, build-time only, dropped by `ComingUpAssembler.enrichTide`); `dayRangesOf` derives their ranges at the run's representative, flags them `shoulder` and drops one it cannot derive; the "fewer than two derivable days" rule counts run days only. A shoulder is ranked but never `peak`, and may be taller than the run's peak (the run is decided on roster high-water height, the bar shows the representative's range). A near miss with no run beside it emits nothing. `buildDayChips` draws the flagged days as outlined bars (no "top N%" pill, no PEAK), drops one before today, and the row note reads "outlined · just short of spring".

**Coming up and the light (tide-light-pro-plan.md B2):** the tide-run card's sunrise/sunset high-water rows (`ComingUpFact.Kind.EVENT_WATER`, set by `ComingUpAssembler.eventWaterFacts`) and the coincidence line's aligned-HW clock are Pro and Admin only. `AlmanacController` serves Lite `ComingUpRoleShaper.forLite(feed)` (rows dropped, `factsLabel` swapped for the in-memory `liteFactsLabel`, `lightRowsWithheld` set); the cached feed stays role-free and Ask reads it unshaped.

**Hot-topic glance line** (`docs/engineering/coming-up-glance-plan.md` §2, phase B1): `HotTopic.glance` is a `GlanceLine(value, shortValue, badge, weather)`, the second line of a Coming up chip, served so the client never composes it per type (null on the wire when unset; cached JSON without it still deserialises). Two writers, neither re-deriving the other's number: `InversionHotTopicStrategy` (`dawn · N/10`) and `MistHotTopicStrategy.enrich` (`{dawn|dusk} · likely, n site(s)`) stamp their own where they hold the figure; `HotTopicGlanceDecorator.decorate` fills every other topic and never overwrites a non-null glance. Surge prints `surgeRun.peak` as served and puts PEAK on every surge topic dated the date with the largest `max(surgeMetres)` (earliest wins a tie); tides print `range` (`· run starts` on day 1 of a multi-day run) with PEAK on `tideRun.peak`; the supermoon prints `rise HH:mm` from the `rises` fact (never `eventTime`) with FULL. ⚠️ **The seam is the end of `HotTopicAggregator.getHotTopics`, in both branches, after the enricher and the travel filter.** `BriefingService` caches the aggregator's output and keeps the cache on a serve only when `Objects.equals(cached.hotTopics(), live)`; decorating anywhere else makes that check always false or leaves the persisted copy without a glance. The first serve after deploy rebuilds once, because pre-B1 cached copies lack the field. `WEATHER_TYPES` (one constant on the decorator) is the single list of weather-driven types.

**Hot topic inputs are read once per serve (performance review P1a).** `HotTopicAggregator.getHotTopics` builds one `HotTopicInputs` (lazy, memoised, unmodifiable, single-thread, never shared across requests) holding `slotSignals()` (the four `SlotSignalReader.read` queries) and the `coastalLocations()`, `enabledLocations()` (ordered by name), `darkSkyLocations()` and `bluebellLocations()` rosters, plus a per-date `isTravelDay`. It calls `HotTopicStrategy.detect(from, to, inputs)` on every strategy, passes the same inputs to `HotTopicEventEnricher.enrich(topics, inputs)` in both the real and the simulation branch, and uses `inputs.isTravelDay` in the travel filter. A strategy that reads slot signals or a roster overrides the three-argument form and reads only from `inputs`; its two-argument `detect` builds a fresh `HotTopicInputs` from its own repositories and delegates, so direct callers keep working. A new strategy must read through `inputs`, never call `SlotSignalReader` or a roster query itself. `HotTopicInputsEquivalenceTest` pins the output and the statement count.
