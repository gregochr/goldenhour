PhotoCast · map tab · verdict & picks

Three ways to put the verdict and the week's picks on a map

The map draws one window at a time and colours it by per-location score, which is why it never says the thing the Plan tab says on every card: Worth it, Maybe, Poor, and which window is the Best bet. The verdict is easy — one reduction of the field, stated once. The picks are the hard half, because a pick is a window and a region together, so on a map showing one window the answer is often “yes, but not this one”. These three options differ in where that fact lives: on the control, on a rail, or on the ground. All three land in the same drilldown — window → region → location — so the choice is about entry, not about three architectures.

What was missing, preciselyThe map had a scale but no sentence. Poor → Worth it under the ramp is a per-location legend, and a screen full of amber does not add up to a verdict, because the verdict is a reduction someone has to perform. So the map now performs it: the verdict is the strongest region's average for that window — the same reduction the Plan tab makes for its cards, which is what stops the two tabs disagreeing about a night.
The picks are the hard halfBest bet and Also good are properties of a (window, region) pair. A map shows one window, so half of every pick is off-screen by construction, and the missing half is the one that matters: the best night of the week is not the one you are looking at. That is the sentence each option has to carry, and it is why none of them can be a badge that only appears when you happen to land on the right window.
Option A · on the controlThe window pill already names the window, so it states the answer for it: verdict word on the right, a verdict-tinted left edge, and the medallion — ◎ BEST BET — when the window it names is a pick. The two steppers gain a verdict tick, so ‹ › stops being blind: you can see that tomorrow is greener before you spend the tap. Picks belonging to other windows sit beside the pill as chips naming their window and region, and tapping one goes there. Cheapest option, no new furniture, and it reads at a glance — but it holds the week's shape in only two dots.
Option B · the week railOne tile per window along the bottom: bar height is the verdict, colour is the tier, medallions ride the corner. The stepper answers next; it never answers which — and choosing a night is a comparison, so the comparison becomes the control. You can see the week turn over: two green nights, a red Thursday, tide topics clustered on the coast windows. Costs about 100px of map and duplicates what the pill said, which is why the pill hides in this option rather than saying it twice.
Option C · on the groundA pick is a place as much as a time, so it is pinned to the region it names, and the pill drops to a single verdict dot because the ground is carrying the words. A pick belonging to another window is drawn dashed and says Show this window › — the honest form of “not this one”. Strongest at making the answer feel spatial; weakest when the two picks are near each other or the map is zoomed into a headland, where the flags leave the viewport and the sentence goes with them.
One drilldown, three entrancesEvery option lands in the same two panels: window → region (each region with its verdict word, 4★+ hit count, nearest drive and best score) then region → location (top locations with drive and leave-by, plus the region's narrative for that window). The location level is the sheet that already exists, so this adds exactly one level rather than a parallel hierarchy. Entry differs — A from the pill menu's footer row, B from a second tap on the current tile, C from a flag — the destination does not.
Filters must not move the verdictVerdict and picks ignore minimum rating, reach, subject and dark-sky. Hiding 3★ locations cannot be allowed to turn a Maybe into a Worth it — the filters decide what the map draws, not what the night is. Scope does apply: My area versus Whole catalogue changes which regions are candidates at all, so it changes both the verdict and the picks, and the panel says so.
Ranked by average, not by ceilingA single 5★ in an otherwise flat region is a lucky location, not a good night. Regions rank on the mean and the row shows the ceiling beside it, so 4 of 31 at 4★+ and 5★ best can be read together — which is the case where the region is worth a drive only if you are going to that one spot.
Open question · what the medallion competes withOn the real screen the pill sits next to the handover breadcrumb from the Plan tab, which already carries a window name. If you arrive from a Plan card that was the best bet, the medallion is a third statement of the same fact. Options: suppress it on handover, or let the crumb carry it and keep the pill to the verdict.
Open question · the verdict word on a mixed nightThursday reads Poor across County Durham and Worth it in the Highlands. With My area on, the Highlands are out of scope and the word is Poor — correct for a plan, misleading for a catalogue. C is the only option that shows the disagreement rather than resolving it, which may be the argument for keeping the flags whatever else is chosen.
