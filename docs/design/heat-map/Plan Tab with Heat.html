The heat summary and the movable origin, in one Plan tab

Coverage is about to grow to Scotland and the Peak District, so the field is framed by drive time rather than a region list — anything under three hours is in the glance, the Highlands are one search away. Two threads joined. The six thumbnails under the lens are a visual index of the window list — the shape of the week before a word is read. The origin chip in the masthead is the frame of reference everything else depends on: search returns days, regions and locations, and picking a region re-points the whole plan at it — pool, drive times, lens label, and the heat itself, which zooms to that region across all six windows. So a region is Plan with the origin moved, not a second view. Try lakes, then tuesday sunrise.

Origin re-points the heat, not just the listPlan from the Lake District and all six thumbnails zoom to the Lakes — six windows of one region, in the same shape. That is why origin belongs here rather than in a second view: the picture is the thing that has to move, and it does.
The strip stays chronologicalOrder · Best re-ranks the rows and numbers them, but never the thumbnails. Reordering the strip would destroy its time axis, which is the only reason the shape of the week is legible at a glance. So the strip is when, the rows are whichever you asked for, and a thumbnail click still opens its row.
Reach relabels, never liesAway from home the lens reads Drive from Keswick and the numbers are local minutes — 22 to Buttermere, not the 172 from your house. Leave-by times recompute from the same figure, so an away plan is actionable rather than decorative.
Drive time decides the frame, not a region listA region is in the undrilled field if you could plausibly drive to it — under three hours. Northern England, the Scottish Borders and the Peak District all clear that; the Highlands do not, so adding Skye to the catalogue cannot flatten the glance to three green pixels. What sits beyond is named under the strip and is one search away, so coverage can grow without the summary degrading.
The frame sets the aspectThumbnails take their proportions from whatever is in scope, so a taller planning area is drawn taller rather than letterboxed into a corner. Drill into one region and every map tightens onto it.
Two scopes, one gestureThe origin chip sets the global frame; the rail under an open window filters that window. When origin is already a single region the rail drops away, because there is nothing left to choose — the whole plan is that region.
The drill-down obeys the lensCards are the intersection of region, rating floor and travel time, and every window footer names the three in force — so a short list reads as "I asked for this", never as "the forecast is bad".
The heat is what the lens does not touchThe field paints the forecast for every rated location, reachable or not. Filtering it per driver would make the same night look different to two people and hide that the Lakes are on fire but two hours away.
