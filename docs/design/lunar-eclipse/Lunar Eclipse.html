Lunar eclipse — plan and screens

The solar eclipse added an ECLIPSE almanac type with a promoted strip, an enriched pill and a west-horizon site score. A lunar eclipse fits the same slots, but three things change the design. The shadow's depth is the same from every location, so the only site question is where the moon sits. It happens before dawn, so you have to decide the evening before. And the problem is timing: the moon is racing toward the horizon as the sky brightens behind you, so the new component on these screens is a timeline of that race.

Worked example: the deep partial lunar eclipse of Friday 28 August 2026. 93% of the moon's diameter enters the umbra at 05:13 BST, and from the North East the moon sets while still in shadow.

NEWThe dawn race. A horizontal timeline from 03:00 to moonset. The twilight gradient is the masthead's light rule, reused. The rose band is the time in the umbra, a hatch shows it continuing below the horizon, and three markers show maximum, sunrise and moonset. It answers the one question the figures above it cannot: how much sky colour is left at maximum, and how long you have between light enough for a foreground and the moon setting. Everything else is the solar strip with different values. The kicker adds tomorrow, because this is the first strip to promote the evening before.

06Catalog and data
Type
ECLIPSE, source ALMANAC, with a new body: 'sun' | 'moon'. It is one type because both use the same scorer, strip and accent. The body field switches the safety line, the glyph and the dawn race.
Accent
--ecl #C4787F, shared with solar. The two eclipses read as one family, and the rose is already close to the copper of an eclipsed moon.
Glyph
🌘 on card pills and Coming up, matching the emoji set those surfaces use. ◑ in mono text lines.
Event data
Stored once per event, not per region: umbralMagnitude, kind: partial|total|penumbral, contacts{P1,U1,U2?,max,U3?,U4,P4} in UTC. Seeded from an ephemeris table, never computed at runtime.
Per location
moonAltAtMax, moonAzAtMax, moonset, and the existing civil dawn / sunrise. These are cheap and derived at forecast time from coordinates, which is what redraws the dawn race when you pick a location.
Eligibility
The moon must be at least 3° up at maximum, or at least 3° up for 30 minutes in the umbra. Penumbral-only eclipses never raise a topic, because the dimming is too faint to photograph as an event.
Site scoring
Reuses the solar eclipse's horizon-obstruction test on a new bearing. It outputs clearToDeg, shown as clear to N°. That is the second event to use it, and the case for building it once.
Promotion
At T−1, 18:00, for any almanac event whose maximum falls between 00:00 and the next sunrise.
Map
Pins only: sites with clearToDeg ≤ 3 on the bearing, with a thin ray drawn at 244° from each. No shading, because magnitude does not vary.
Weather
Cloud is the only uncertainty, and only in the WSW sector below 15°. The verdict uses low-cloud cover on the bearing, not whole-sky cloud. The same cloud rule applies to the solar version.
Calls for you
  1. Promotion at T−1. The solar handoff left this open. A pre-dawn eclipse makes it unavoidable, so this plan treats it as decided for night events.
  2. Does the dawn race belong on the strip, or only in the popup? On the strip it makes the promo the tallest thing on Plan the evening before. In the popup only, the strip goes back to the solar layout and the race is one tap away.
  3. Totals. The next UK-visible total is 31 Dec 2028, at moonrise in the evening. The race works the same way reversed (moonrise, then dusk), but the strip needs a totality figure and an extra band for U2 to U3. Design it now, or when it enters the 90-day window?
  4. Can it be the Best bet? The pick system ranks on light. On the mock, Friday sunrise wins anyway. If the light scored poorly, should a clear WSW horizon with an eclipse lift it?
