One row, not a wrapping gridSpots are a film strip now: three and a half cards visible, the half one faded at the edge to say there is more, arrows and a "7 of 18 loaded" readout in the footer. A wrapping grid implied the list had ended at four and left ragged empty cells; a strip says the opposite, and the whole loaded set is reachable without leaving the window.
Region, not type, on the cardThe type word was a leftover from the filter it belonged to — it told you Blyth Beach is coast, which you knew. The region tells you which way you are driving and matches the Best Bet's own region line, so it goes back in that slot. Type survives in the drilldown, next to the control that uses it.
No invented vocabulary"Spring tide run 2 of 3" asked you to learn a word and count something you cannot see. It now says the biggest tides of the month, names the three nights, marks which is largest, and explains why in one clause. "11 aligned" is gone too — a count of alignments is a number about the data, not about tonight.
One control on the page, three in the drilldownThe bar holds only reach, because reach is the only thing that changes today's decision. Rating is already the sort order and location type is a word on each card. Click All N for this window and the browsing filters appear where you are actually browsing — reach, rating floor, type — over the whole list rather than the top four.
Pre-pilot cut: one plain control"Close to home · 30 mi" became How far tonight · 45 min / 1h 30 / 2h 30 — time, because that is how the decision is actually made, and the miles are on every card anyway. The explainer link is gone: nothing left to explain. Reach is stated once, in the control, and the rail no longer repeats it.
Pre-pilot cut: nothing depends on historySignal decay keeps the half that needs no memory — copy that states the delta ("clearest in 11 nights", "biggest tides of the month"), computed from the data alone. The per-user exposure counter is out until after the pilot: five friends reporting bugs need to be looking at the same page on the same night, and silence must never mean "you have been told before".
With users, generation stops being personalScoring is per site and window, not per person — so 4 regions × 2 windows × 4 days is generated once and serves everyone. Your home address and calendar only decide what you are shown: drive times cached per user, reach as a lens, away days muted. The one genuinely per-user cost is the written briefing, and even that is regional prose — everyone in the Dales shares tonight's Best Bet paragraph. Marginal cost per user stays near flat, which is the commercially interesting part.
Availability is all a user needs to seeAway days are marked in the rail — muted with the forecast still there once you have users, not generated at all while you are solo. Coverage, spend and the regions/windows levers stay in the existing Manage tab; none of it belongs in a normal user's view.
Hover peeks, click opens the mapHovering a spot after 140ms gives the why: rating, drive, one clause of reasoning, and the two scores — framed with header and footer so it reads as a panel, not a glitch. Clicking goes straight to the map centred on that spot with its window preserved. On the phone there is no hover, so a tap opens the same peek full-width and its footer link carries you to the map.
