Why the heat looked like it was in the seaIt partly was. The kernel is a gaussian centred on each rated location, so a coastal spot spreads inland and out to sea equally — and inland it lands on dark land tiles while out to sea it lands on darker water, which reads as the data is offshore. The Plan thumbnails never had the problem because they clip to real coastline. The tile host now builds the same UK land mask once per zoom, in absolute pixel coordinates, and slides it with the pan, so clipping costs a translate rather than a re-projection. The kernel radius also came down from 8.5km to 7.2km with a lower cap.
…and how the fix overcorrectedClipping to that mask raw erased seven coastal locations completely, Marsden Bay's 5★ among them: Natural Earth 1:50m carries 2–5km of coastline error, which is further offshore than a coastal spot sits, so the clip removed the blob's own core. A rated 5★ rendering as empty ground is worse than the original complaint, because empty ground reads as nothing here. Two things fix it. The mask is grown seaward by about 4km — a uniform band that follows the coast, where an earlier attempt to union a disc at each location drew conspicuous circles offshore (the error is geographic, so the disc had to grow with zoom). And the mask is applied as a blurred alpha mask rather than a hard clip, so the boundary feathers at the same rate the field does instead of putting a crisp line through a soft wash. Measured: all 51 locations paint, minimum alpha 154 of 255. For production, swap in a real UK coastline and the seaward slack can go to nearly zero.
Three controls said "when"A seven-day date strip, four event pills, and an in-map Tonight Sunset · 19:58 select. Two of them were the same fact and the third was that fact again with a time on it. One list replaces all three: every event in the forecast, in the order it happens, with each night's astro and aurora sitting after that day's sunset because that is when they are. Aurora only appears on a night the forecast flags it — an always-visible Aurora tab that is empty six nights in seven teaches people to ignore it.
The filter block was bigger than the mapMinimum quality, subject, logistics and the admin row occupied more vertical than the 500px map underneath them. They are all still here, in a popover whose chip carries the active count, and the map now owns the whole frame with no page scroll. The two controls you actually touch every visit — the window and Heat/Pins — stay on the surface.
Selection happens on the mapA Leaflet popup takes the thing you just selected and covers it. Now the point gets a ring and the callout tails into it, so the answer stays in the place you asked about — and the field, the region names and the neighbouring locations stay readable around it. The callout carries the verdict for this window, the reason, drive and leave-by, the night's topics, and every window for that one location, so you can compare Bamburgh across the week without leaving the map.
No search box hereThe Plan tab's field is for choosing an origin, which is a Plan question. On a map, panning is the search, and a text box in the masthead invites you to type a place you can already see. What the map needs is a jump list, and there are only ever a handful of regions — so ◎ Regions names each one with its drive time and this window's best score, and jumping to one outside your area switches scope for you rather than refusing.
Names, from the Plan tabLocation chips are the same component as the Plan popup's: a square in the ramp colour, the name, and this window's rating, placed greedily best-first and dropped rather than overlapped. Region names claim space first while the field is the subject, then hand over as you zoom — past a county the question stops being where and becomes which, and a smear cannot answer which.
The home marker and the ringsEvery drive time and leave-by on this screen is measured from DH3 4NG, so the map states it: a marker, and dashed rings at 45 minutes and 1h 30 while you are zoomed out. That is what turns a warm blob into an hour north of me. The masthead postcode is a statement of that fact, not a control.
The ground it sits onBasemap changed after comparing six on the same field (Map Basemap Options.html). Now Esri Dark Gray, warmed into the palette by a CSS filter. It is not label-free: the base tile still sets country and water-body names — SCOTLAND, Solway Firth, Firth of Clyde — but no towns, which is the class that was competing with our location chips for the same pixels, and ours carry a rating theirs cannot. Town names come back as a reference layer we control past zoom 11.8, where our chips thin out and the village you are driving through starts being useful. The coast is stroked from the same geometry the field is clipped to, so the ground keeps its shape on a basemap quiet enough to let the field lead. CARTO also now watermarks its no-label styles without a key, and the shipped app already credits Esri.
No ramp colour as textStar numerals are never drawn in the ramp colour. As a fill the ramp colour has ink placed on top of it and the ink can be chosen per fill to pass; as text the ramp colour is the ink and cannot. The mid-peaked luminance makes it unfixable by a lightness floor, because 1★ and 5★ fail from opposite ends of the ramp — measured, ramp-as-text gave 1★ 2.37 and 5★ 3.04 against their own chip grounds. So the swatch or the badge fill carries the colour and the numeral takes #FFFFFF or #0F172A, picked per fill by HeatField.ink(). Every star label on this screen now measures ≥ 5.56:1. Rule from design_temp_scale, Change 5a.
The scale, and the bloomThe field is coloured by the shipped temperature scale — cold slate blue at 1★ through gold to deep red at 5★ — not the old red/amber/green verdict ramp. That change inverts the basemap polarity: the hot end is now the ramp's darkest colour by design, so under plain compositing on a dark ground the gold middle is the brightest thing on screen and the map is loudest where the night is merely average. A blend mode cannot fix it (screen and lighter are monotonic in source luminance). What does is a second emissive layer whose alpha follows the score, gated from 3★ up and composited additively inside the overlay canvas — so hot glows because it is hot. Measured at the blob core over this basemap, brightness now climbs 3★ 88 · 4★ 101 · 4.5★ 107 · 5★ 117 where it used to fall. The gate stays at 3★ on every surface: 3★ is where the ramp's own luminance peaks, so a higher gate leaves a dead band in which the ramp darkens and the bloom has not started. See Light or Dark Map.html.
Pins kept as the honest comparisonOne dot per location, no field. It is why heat became the default: at anything wider than a county, clusters average their own gems away. It stays one tap away rather than being argued about.
Names ramp with zoom, over what is in viewAt the regional glance, the best location in each region plus the strongest few in the viewport — about a dozen names, because thirty is a legend and the field is the subject at that width. By county scale the local set has taken over, and closer than that everything named in view gets a chip. Candidates are always sorted best first and a name that cannot find clear air is dropped rather than stacked, so what you lose zooming out is always the weakest.
Open question · subject tagsThe catalogue here carries coast and lake honestly; Woodland, Waterfall and Wildlife are derived for the prototype so the chip row behaves like the shipped one. In the port they come off the location record, and the filter logic is unchanged.
Open question · the third night eventAstro is scored here from darkness and clarity, aurora from latitude, darkness and Kp. Both are plausible and neither is the shipped model. If the real astro score is only defined for dark-sky locations, the event list should say so on the row rather than silently thinning the map.
