What is actually wrong todayOn the current dark map the gold 3★ middle is the brightest thing on the screen and the deep red 5★ sinks toward the basemap. So the map is loudest where the night is merely average and quietest where it is worth driving for. That is not a preference, it is the ordering being backwards — and it only appeared when the scale changed from RAG to cold-to-hot.
The recommendation: stay dark, add a heat bloomA blend mode cannot fix this, and it is worth being clear why: screen and lighter are monotonic in source luminance, so they lift the whole field and leave the ranking exactly where it was. A dark red will never outrank a light gold under either. What works is a second emissive layer whose alpha follows the score, gated from 3★ up — hot areas then glow brightest because they are hot, not because of what their colour happens to weigh. The stops stay frozen, the night feel stays, and the luminance climb from 3★ to 5★ becomes monotonic again: 152, 153, 165, 189. Cell 2.
Light is the honest alternative — with one flawDeep red on pale grey is the highest-contrast thing on the map with no compositing trick at all, and the gold middle recedes on its own. But because the ramp peaks in the middle, both ends separate from a pale ground: the cold blue reads as a heavy grey smudge over the Lakes and Dales, so “don't bother” draws the eye almost as hard as “go”. Cell 4 shows it warmed into the palette, which is worth looking at before rejecting light for clashing. The other costs are an inverted label style to maintain, and a daylight instrument for a thing you use at dusk.
Do not fix this by changing the rampThe obvious move — lighten the hot end so it pops on dark — is ruled out upstream. design_temp_scale froze the stops with the hot leg descending in luminance specifically so 4.3★ cannot read hotter than 5★, and it narrowed the marker sub-AA runs from 13.2% to 10.2%. Re-lightening the top would reintroduce both problems to solve a compositing question.
What the bloom costsIt is a second pass over the same grid, so the field paint roughly doubles in cost — still cheap, because the grid is 6px cells and the pass is a plain alpha write. The bloom is multiplied by forecast confidence so a day-4 guess cannot glow as hard as tonight, and it is gated at 3★ so nothing in the cold or middle half is touched. Worth confirming on the real screen that a cluster of adjacent 4★ locations does not accumulate into a false 5★.
The prototype was on the wrong scaleSeparately: design/heat-field.js still carried the old RAG stops, so every map I have shown you this session was coloured by a scale the app has already moved off. It now carries the real STOPS_TEMP — uneven spacing and descending hot leg intact — which is why the Map tab looks different from the screenshots earlier in this conversation.
