The pills move onto the pictures, and the row list goes away

Same data, one storey shallower. Each of the six windows states its own verdict on its own thumbnail — verdict, Best bet or Also good, and any hot topic on that night — so the list of six rows that used to repeat all of it is deleted, and with it the accordion, the second heat map, and the four ways a window told you it was Maybe. The frames take the vertical the list was using, laid out as a matrix: a column per day, sunrise row above sunset row, so reading down is one day and reading across compares the same light day to day. Two cells are empty by definition — this morning has gone, the last evening is past the end of the forecast — and they say so rather than being closed up. Everything that lived inside an open row now opens as one popup on the picture you clicked: the big field, the region pills, tide, and the ranked locations. The postcode is stated once, in the light rule, which is also the search trigger and the origin control.

The verdict belongs on the pictureA thumbnail carries the day, the arrow, the field, the time, its verdict, whether it is the pick, and any hot topic on that night. That is the whole judgement, so the row underneath had nothing left to say — it was restating the pill in four different ways. Six pictures, six verdicts, no list.
The per-card delta earned nothingA ▲0.3 chip on every card was a third statement of movement: the line under the strip already names the two windows that actually moved, in words, with the region that moved them, and the popup header states it with the run age beside it. Nobody drives somewhere over three tenths of a star, so the card is quieter without it. If that top-right slot wants filling later, the honest candidate is the window's best spot — Bamburgh 5★ — not a trend arrow.
The drill-down is a popup, not a storeyRegion pills, narrative, tide and ranked locations open over the plan rather than pushing it down the page. You come back to exactly the view you left, which the accordion could never promise: opening row four used to move rows five and six.
What this deletesThe six-row list, the row-level accordion, the second heat map per row, Order · When / Best, and the duplicate postcode. The strip was always chronological, so an order control that only re-ranked the rows had nothing left to re-rank.
One postcode, in the ruleThe light rule already says Home · DH3 4NG beside today's blue and golden times, so the origin chip below it was saying the same thing twice. Now the tick line is the origin control: it names where the plan is computed from, and it opens search.
Picking a region swaps words, not furnitureThe prose slot under the pills is always there at the same size — unpicked it reads the window as a whole, picked it reads that region. The panel it replaces appeared on selection and shoved tide and locations down the popup, which made a filter feel like a navigation. Counts stay on the pill, the filters stay in the footer, and the All regions pill is the clear.
The grade tints the surfaceFollowing the regional planner, the verdict now washes the whole card rather than sitting in a chip: green for Worth it, amber for Maybe, red-brown for Poor, at around a tenth opacity so it never competes with the field inside it. The word stays in the same column in its grade colour, and the pick is still the border, so three signals coexist without any of them being a badge.
The pick rides the border, the verdict stays putSet inside the card, BEST BET had to fight the verdict word for the same slot and lost — smaller type, same green, and it displaced the verdict entirely. Now it sits in the border line like a legend on a fieldset, so the frame that already marks the card gets named, and all six cards keep a verdict in the same column. Exactly one window is BEST BET and one is ALSO GOOD: a recommendation that fires on half the week is not a recommendation.
The week is a grid, not a queueFour day columns by sunrise and sunset on desktop and iPad; the phone transposes the same markup — day heading across the row, its two windows beneath — and gives a day with only one window the full width. Because every card is stacked, the record has the whole card width, so a name like St Mary's Lighthouse fits on one line. The popup is a centred card on desktop and iPad, and full-screen on phone.
Two numbers were one number twiceThe average is the verdict word — same figure, rounded to “Worth it” — and a bare ceiling read 5★ on four of six cards, so a two-row table discriminated nothing. In its place, one row that names a place: the best spot you could actually reach, its rating in the ramp colour, and the spread bars beside it — 1★→5★ counts, so a lone spike on the right reads one good spot, drive to it and a right-weighted block reads the whole area is on. Hover the bars for exact counts, or the name for its region, drive and leave-by.
Topic eligibility is derived, not declaredA king tide is only true where there is tide, and an inversion needs valleys to pool fog in — so each topic names a location property it needs (coast, lake) and a region qualifies only if it holds a named location with it. Aurora and NLC carry no requirement, because at this latitude the whole planning area sees them or none of it does. The consequence is that moving the origin edits the topic list by itself: plan from the Lake District and the tide topic drops out, with no per-region topic table to maintain. The popup row states the scope and how many regions currently qualify.
More than one topic on a nightEvery topic is named on the card. A night carrying three — noctilucent season over a king tide with dust in the air — is the most interesting night of the week, so collapsing two of them into +2 behind a hover had it backwards, and a phone has no hover at all. The line wraps into the room the card has spare, ordered rarest first: aurora, NLC, king tide, inversion, dust. The popup still carries each one in full with its science note.
Hot topics ride the window they happen inA topic is true of a night, not of a location, so it sits on the picture: ✦ NLC on tonight, 🌌 Aurora across Wednesday night's pair, ☁️ Inversion on Friday's mist. The slot is reserved on every card so a topic-free window doesn't shorten its neighbours, and the popup carries the full row — name, detail, region count, and the science behind the i.
The field names places, not just areasThe popup map carries location chips — a square on the point, the name, and that window's rating — so the blob resolves into Bamburgh 5★ rather than "somewhere up the coast". They come from the same filtered pool the cards below are ranked from, so the map can never name a spot the list has excluded. Placement is greedy: regions claim space first, then the strongest locations, and a chip that cannot fit is dropped rather than overlapped. Tap one for its four days.
The field names where you areEvery reading on a card is measured from DH3 4NG, and until now the picture never showed it. A marker and the four area names turn a warm blob into a bearing — north of me, an hour west — and the popup adds dashed rings at 45 min and 1h 30 so the same field says whether tonight's warmth is reachable tonight.
Area names, not place namesFour region names and four location names on one thumbnail is a legend, not a map. The thumbnail answers which part of the country; the card's Best row and the popup's location chips answer where exactly.
Still one heat kernelThumbnails and the popup field both come from heat-field.js with the same bucketing, fitted to whatever the origin has in scope. Nothing about the field maths changed — the frames are simply taller now that the row list is not competing for the page.
Open question for the portThe lens is down to two controls. If reach and rating are the only two, they could move into the tick line beside the postcode and give the plan its whole vertical back — worth trying before the markers get their verdict tokens.
