Where am I, and which way is good?

The heat is legible as a shape but anonymous as a place: nothing on the thumbnail says where DH3 4NG sits, so warmth two hundred pixels north reads the same as warmth twenty minutes up the A1. Five treatments below, all on the same window (Thursday sunset, the best of the week), all drawn by the existing heat-field.js — the only thing that changes is what gets named on top.

Five ways to name the ground
The recommendation, across all six windows
Home is not a label, it is the originEvery other reading on the card — drive time, leave by, reach filter — is measured from one point that the picture never shows. Marking it costs nine pixels and turns the blob into a direction: north of me, an hour west.
Area names or place names, not bothFour region names and four location names on a 300px thumbnail is a legend, not a map. Areas answer "which part of the country"; places answer "where exactly" — and the card already names the single best place in its own Best row, so the thumbnail's job is the area.
The ring is the honest version of reachA dashed circle at the current filter is drawn from real distance, not opinion, and it answers the second half of the question: heat inside the ring is tonight, heat outside it is a day trip. It does mean the picture carries a filter — the one thing the heat has so far refused to do.
Landmarks age better than regionsCoverage is growing to Scotland and the Peak. Region labels multiply with it; Newcastle · Carlisle · Middlesbrough do not, and a photographer reads a coastline off known towns faster than off area names.
Open questionShould the label set thin out with confidence, like the heat does? Day four is a hazy guess — naming four regions on it may lend it more authority than it has earned.
