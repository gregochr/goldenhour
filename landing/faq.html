A little more about how PhotoCast works, what goes into the forecasts, and how it can help you plan your photography.

Whether you're just getting started or like to understand the finer details of the weather, hopefully you'll find the answers here.

What is PhotoCast?

PhotoCast is a forecasting tool designed to help landscape, coastal and wildlife photographers plan around the light.

It looks at weather and atmospheric conditions across more than 200 locations in northern England, assessing the potential for colourful sunrises, sunsets and golden-hour light.

Rather than simply telling you whether it will be cloudy or sunny, PhotoCast helps explain what those conditions might mean for your photographs.

It's not a guarantee of beautiful light, but it can help you make a more informed decision about when and where to go.

Do I need to be an experienced photographer?

Not at all. PhotoCast is designed for anyone who enjoys photographing the landscape, whether you're just starting out or have been doing it for years.

The simple ratings and forecast map give you a quick overview, while the more detailed information is there when you want to explore further.

Can I try PhotoCast without paying?

Yes. PhotoCast Lite is free, and you don't need to provide payment card details to get started.

It gives you access to the forecast map, location ratings and three-day planning information.

If you later decide you'd like more detailed forecasts and additional planning tools, you can explore Pro.

Where can I use PhotoCast?

PhotoCast currently covers more than 200 locations across northern England, including Northumberland, the Lake District, North Yorkshire, the Yorkshire Dales, the North York Moors, Tyne and Wear and Teesdale.

Locations include coastal viewpoints, lakes, mountains, valleys and other popular landscape photography spots.

Which platforms can I use it on?

PhotoCast runs in the browser — that build is very close to ready, and early access is open now. iPad and iPhone apps are coming soon.

What do the star ratings mean?

The star ratings are a quick guide to how promising the forecast conditions look for sunrise or sunset.

Higher ratings suggest better potential for interesting light, while lower ratings indicate that conditions may be less favourable.

They're a guide rather than a guarantee — sometimes an unexpected break in the clouds produces the best photographs.

What is Ask PhotoCast?

Ask PhotoCast lets you ask questions about the forecast in everyday language.

You might want to know why a particular location has a good rating, what the cloud conditions could mean for the light, or whether another location might be worth considering.

Rather than having to interpret all the weather information yourself, you can ask a question and get an explanation to help you make sense of the forecast.

Ask PhotoCast is available with Pro.

What's the difference between Lite and Pro?

Lite gives you a free three-day overview of sunrise and sunset conditions, with location ratings, a forecast map and a planning grid.

Pro is designed for photographers who'd like to explore the detail behind those forecasts. It adds more in-depth light analysis, written explanations, recommended locations, Ask PhotoCast, aurora forecasts and additional planning tools.

You can start with Lite and decide later whether Pro would be useful to you.

How often are the forecasts updated?

Sunrise and sunset forecasts are refreshed three times a day — in the early morning, afternoon and evening — using the latest available weather information.

Aurora conditions can change much more quickly, so PhotoCast checks space weather data every five minutes during the hours of darkness.

How far ahead can I plan?

PhotoCast provides forecasts for the next three days.

Forecasts are generally more useful closer to the event, so tomorrow's outlook is usually a better guide than one for three days away.

The three-day view gives you enough time to think about where you might go, while recognising that the weather can still change.

How accurate is PhotoCast?

Weather and light are never completely predictable, so PhotoCast can't guarantee a spectacular sunrise or sunset.

It uses weather forecasts, atmospheric data and AI analysis to help identify promising conditions. Forecasts are generally more useful closer to the event, particularly for the following day.

Think of PhotoCast as another tool to help you make an informed decision, rather than a promise of what the sky will do.

Can PhotoCast guarantee a good sunrise or sunset?

Unfortunately not — and that's part of the joy and frustration of landscape photography.

The atmosphere is constantly changing, and even a promising forecast can turn out differently from what was expected.

PhotoCast aims to help you recognise favourable conditions, not remove the element of surprise.

Where does PhotoCast get its forecasts?

PhotoCast brings together information from several established weather, atmospheric and environmental data sources.

These include detailed cloud forecasts, humidity, wind, airborne particles, sunrise and sunset calculations, tidal predictions and space weather information.

AI helps interpret this information, assess the potential for photographic light and explain the reasoning behind the forecast.

Why is there a paid version?

I'd like PhotoCast to remain useful, reliable and available for the long term.

Running the service involves ongoing costs, including weather data, AI processing and hosting. Pro subscriptions help cover those costs and support further development.

Lite will remain free, so you can use PhotoCast without paying. Pro is there for photographers who find the additional detail and planning tools worthwhile.

There's no obligation to upgrade.

Who's behind PhotoCast?

PhotoCast is an independent project created and maintained by a landscape photography enthusiast with over 25 years of professional software engineering experience.

It began with a simple question: would tomorrow morning's sunrise be worth getting up for?

What started as a personal experiment has grown into a forecasting tool for photographers across northern England.

I'm continuing to develop PhotoCast, improve its forecasts and add features that I hope other photographers will find useful.

Is PhotoCast a safety tool?

No. PhotoCast provides forecasts for planning purposes only. Always check official weather warnings and tide tables before visiting coastal or remote locations. Conditions can change rapidly. You are responsible for your own safety at all times.
