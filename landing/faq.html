Getting started

What is PhotoCast?

PhotoCast helps photographers find promising conditions for sunrise, sunset and golden-hour photography across more than 200 locations in northern England.

It brings together weather and atmospheric information to assess the potential for interesting light, with simple ratings and explanations to help you understand the forecast.

It's not a guarantee of a beautiful sky, but it can help you make a more informed decision about when and where to go.

Do I need to be an experienced photographer?

Not at all. PhotoCast is designed for anyone who enjoys photographing the landscape, whether you're just starting out or have been doing it for years.

The map and star ratings give you a straightforward overview. If you enjoy understanding the finer details, you can explore the atmospheric information behind the forecasts.

Do I need to understand weather charts?

Not at all. The map and star ratings give you a simple overview, even if you're new to landscape photography.

Can I try PhotoCast without paying?

Yes. PhotoCast Lite is free, and you don't need to provide payment card details to get started.

Lite gives you the forecast map, the planning grid for the next few days and star ratings for up to 3 sub-regions you choose.

PhotoCast is in early access, so places are limited for now. If they're full when you sign up, you can leave your email address and join the waitlist.

If you later decide you'd like more detail and planning tools, you can explore Pro.

Which areas does PhotoCast cover?

PhotoCast currently covers more than 200 locations across northern England, including Northumberland, the Lake District, North Yorkshire, the Yorkshire Dales, the North York Moors, Tyne and Wear and Teesdale.

Locations include coastal viewpoints, lakes, mountains, valleys and other popular landscape photography spots.

Which platforms can I use it on?

PhotoCast is available through your web browser, on your computer, tablet or phone. Early access is open now.

Dedicated iPad and iPhone apps are in development.

Understanding the forecasts

What do the star ratings mean?

Each sunrise and sunset at each location gets a rating from one to five stars for its photographic potential.

One or two stars means the light is unlikely to reward a trip — a thick overcast, for example. Three stars means some colour is possible but not reliable. Four stars means conditions look good and worth the effort. Five stars is kept for the rare mornings and evenings when everything looks set to come together.

Ratings look at more than the amount of cloud: its height, where it sits relative to the sun, the moisture and particles in the air, and how conditions are developing. Where conditions are clearly unpromising — heavy low cloud towards the sun, rain, or poor visibility — PhotoCast says so without a full assessment.

Higher ratings mean more promising conditions, not a guaranteed photograph.

What's the difference between Fiery Sky and Golden Hour?

Alongside the star rating, Pro shows two scores out of 100 for each location.

Fiery Sky looks at the potential for colourful skies around sunrise and sunset. It depends on mid- and high-level cloud being in the right place to catch the light, so a completely clear sky scores modestly and a heavy overcast scores low.

Golden Hour considers the conditions for warm, directional light that can bring shape and depth to the landscape. A clear sky with good visibility can score well here even when there's little chance of a colourful sky.

The two often differ, which is part of the point: a morning that's poor for colour can still be good for light on the land.

How far ahead can I see forecasts?

Star ratings cover today and the next three days, so the planning views cover up to four days. Today and tomorrow are always assessed. The two days after that are rated when the weather is settled enough for a rating to mean something; when it isn't, PhotoCast leaves them unrated rather than guess.

Forecasts become more reliable as the event gets closer, so tomorrow's outlook is usually a better guide than one for three days away.

Coming up looks further ahead, at events you can plan around well in advance, such as notable tides and eclipses.

How often are forecasts updated?

Sunrise and sunset forecasts are updated three times a day: an overnight run covering the next few days, an afternoon refresh of tonight's sunset and tomorrow's sunrise and sunset, and an evening refresh of tomorrow's sunrise.

Aurora conditions can change much more quickly, so PhotoCast checks space weather data every five minutes, watching tonight's outlook during the day and current conditions after dark.

It's worth checking again the evening before, or shortly before you set off.

How accurate are the forecasts?

Weather and light are never completely predictable, so PhotoCast can't guarantee a spectacular sunrise or sunset.

Forecasts are generally most useful close to the event, particularly for the following morning or evening.

Can PhotoCast guarantee a good sunrise or sunset?

No — and that's part of the joy and frustration of landscape photography.

Even a promising forecast can turn out differently, and sometimes an unexpected break in the cloud produces the best photograph of the year. PhotoCast aims to help you recognise favourable conditions, not remove the element of surprise.

Features and subscriptions

What is Ask PhotoCast?

Ask PhotoCast lets you ask questions about the forecast in everyday language.

You might want to understand why a location has a particular rating, what the cloud conditions mean for the light, or whether conditions favour a particular kind of photograph. You can also ask follow-up questions.

It uses the forecast information PhotoCast already has to give straightforward explanations, helping you understand what's behind the ratings.

A few questions it handles well:

  • Best spot this weekend?
  • Best coastal spot at high tide?
  • Anything for the night sky tonight?
  • Any rare events coming up?

Available with PhotoCast Pro.

How do I choose my sub-regions?

Each part of the coast, hills and dales PhotoCast covers is split into smaller sub-regions. With Lite, you get star ratings for up to 3 of them, and you choose which.

You pick them when you sign up, or the first time you sign in. The first week is free to change, so you can try different ones. After that you can add sub-regions freely while you're under your limit. Swapping or removing one can be done once every 30 days.

Places outside your sub-regions still appear on the map, just without their ratings. Pro covers every sub-region.

What's the difference between Lite and Pro?

Lite gives you a free overview of sunrise and sunset conditions over the next few days, with a forecast map, a planning grid and location ratings for up to 3 sub-regions you choose.

Pro gives you ratings for every sub-region, and adds deeper forecast analysis, Ask PhotoCast, location recommendations and additional tools for photographers who want to explore the conditions in more detail. That includes separate Fiery Sky and Golden Hour scores, explanations for individual locations, aurora forecasts, the full tide picture at the light — water level, tide curve and coastal locations matched to the tide — and personalised drive times.

Ratings at coastal locations take the tide into account for everyone. Lite also shows the state of the tide at sunrise and sunset, and highlights notable tides such as spring tides.

Pro pricing will be announced before paid subscriptions begin.

You can start with Lite and decide later whether Pro would be useful to you.

Why is there a paid version?

I'd like PhotoCast to remain useful, reliable and available for the long term.

Running the service involves ongoing costs, including weather data, AI processing and hosting. Pro subscriptions help cover those costs and support further development.

Lite will remain free, so you can use PhotoCast without paying. Pro is there for photographers who find the additional detail and planning tools worthwhile.

There's no obligation to upgrade.

Data sources and technical details

Where does PhotoCast get its forecasts?

In short: PhotoCast starts with the same kind of weather forecast you'd find elsewhere, then looks more closely at the parts that matter for light.

That means which layers of cloud are forecast and where they sit relative to the rising or setting sun, how much moisture and dust is in the air, and where the sun will be. Near the coast it adds the tides; at night, space weather for the northern lights.

PhotoCast weighs these together to judge how promising a sunrise or sunset looks, and explains its reasoning in plain language. Anthropic's Claude helps with that assessment and with the written explanations.

About PhotoCast

Who's behind PhotoCast?

PhotoCast is an independent project created and maintained by a landscape photography enthusiast with over 25 years of professional software engineering experience.

It began with a simple question: would tomorrow morning's sunrise be worth getting up for?

What started as a personal experiment has grown into a forecasting tool for photographers across northern England.

I'm continuing to develop PhotoCast, improve its forecasts and add features that I hope other photographers will find useful.

Is PhotoCast a safety tool?

No. PhotoCast provides forecasts for planning purposes only. Always check official weather warnings and tide tables before visiting coastal or remote locations. Conditions can change rapidly. You are responsible for your own safety at all times.
