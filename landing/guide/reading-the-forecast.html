Guide/Reading the forecast

Reading the
forecast.

The same few ideas appear on every tab: star ratings, a short verdict, the colour on the map and a handful of markings. This page helps you understand what each of them is telling you.

Star ratings

Every rated location gets between one and five stars for a particular sunrise or sunset. The stars describe the potential for photographic light, not the chance of a clear sky. Some cloud in the right place often scores higher than a cloudless sky.

One or two stars means the light is unlikely to reward a trip, such as under a thick overcast. Three stars means some colour is possible but not reliable. Four stars means conditions look good and worth the effort. Five stars is kept for the rare mornings and evenings when everything looks set to come together.

A forecast window is a particular sunrise, sunset or night, and a rating always belongs to one of them. The same headland can be two stars at sunrise and four stars at sunset, so check which sunrise or sunset you're looking at.

They're a guide rather than a guarantee. Sometimes an unexpected break in the cloud produces the best photographs of the year.

Verdicts

Each sunrise and sunset also gets a short verdict, so you can see the shape of the next few days before reading any detail. The same three verdicts and colours are used everywhere in the app.

Worth it
Conditions look promising at some locations. On Pro this can be limited to locations within your chosen drive time.
Maybe
Mixed. There may be something, but it's not a strong signal.
Poor
Little sign of interesting light this time.

Colour on the map

The map, and the small maps on At a Glance, paint each rated location's score as colour, blended where locations sit close together. Cool colours mean less promising conditions and warm gold means the most promising.

1★ poor3★5★ worth it

Colour only appears near rated locations, and only over land, so an empty patch of sea or moorland isn't a bad forecast. You can choose a different colour scale in Settings.

Hatching
A faint hatch marks places not yet rated for this sunrise or sunset.
Plain ground
No PhotoCast locations there.
Stood down
A darker veil with hatching: the weather (cloud, rain or poor visibility) has ruled the area out for this sunrise or sunset. The cause is named on the map. Neither kind of hatching is drawn on aurora or astro nights.

Confidence

Forecasts are more reliable closer to the event, so tomorrow is usually a better guide than three days away. PhotoCast shows this rather than hiding it. Confidence depends on how far ahead the forecast is, how much the ratings disagree with each other and how many locations are rated. Where confidence is lower, the colour on the At a Glance maps is drawn more faintly and greyer, and the verdict fades. The lowest confidence is marked as provisional. Later dates are often less certain, but a date is not faded just for being further ahead.

A provisional rating means confidence is lower. A provisional four stars is worth keeping an eye on, so check again after the next forecast update before committing to an early start.

Fixed or could still move

Some things are known well in advance. Tide times, moon phases, meteor shower peaks and eclipses fall on predictable dates. A predictable date is not a guarantee of conditions: cloud may hide a meteor shower, and actual water levels can differ from the predicted tide because of wind and air pressure. Others depend on the weather, such as valley mist, cloud inversions and Saharan dust, and only show up once they're in the forecast.

PhotoCast marks the difference the same way everywhere:

Solid outline
Fixed. The date and time are known in advance and won't change.
Dashed outline
Could still move. It depends on the forecast and may change, or not happen.
WX
Weather-driven. Only known from the forecast, so it appears once the forecast shows it.
PEAK
The peak day of something that runs over several days, such as several days of spring tides.

Hot topics

Hot topics are the conditions and events that make a particular day stand out. They're grouped into families, each with its own colour and symbol, which you'll see as small labels on At a Glance, as filters on Coming up and as dots on the Map.

🌊 Coastal
Spring and king tides, storm surges and big seas.
🌌 Night sky
Aurora, meteor showers and noctilucent cloud. See Aurora and night sky.
☀️ Sun & moon
Supermoons, eclipses, equinoxes and solstices.
🏜️ Air & dust
Saharan dust and other particles that can change the colour of the sky.

Valley mist and cloud inversions are weather-driven, so they appear on At a Glance and the Map once forecast, but not in the 90-day Coming up list. See every topic, with a short description of each.

Fiery Sky and Golden HourPro

The star rating gives you the overview. With Pro you can see two separate assessments behind it for each location:

  • Fiery Sky looks at the potential for colourful skies around sunrise and sunset.
  • Golden Hour considers the conditions for warm, directional light that can bring shape and depth to the landscape.

Both are scores out of 100. They measure different qualities of light, so one can be high while the other is low, and that's useful. A morning with a poor Fiery Sky score can still have lovely Golden Hour light on a hillside.

ProFiery Sky and Golden Hour scores are available with Pro.

Tide words

High and low water
The top and bottom of the tide, shortened to HW and LW. The water level rises towards high water and falls towards low water.
Spring tide
Spring tides have a larger difference between high and low water, with higher highs and lower lows. They occur around new and full moons, for two or three nights; the name does not refer to the season.
King tide
The very biggest spring tides of the year.
Dimmed Pro
A coastal location whose preferred tide level (high, mid or low) doesn't match the predicted tide at sunrise or sunset. It's drawn faded on the map. The star rating already takes the tide into account for everyone, so dimming changes only how the marker is drawn.

Drive times and reach

Once you've set a home postcode, PhotoCast can work out the drive time to every location. "Within reach" then means the locations you could get to inside the drive time you've chosen. Without a postcode PhotoCast still shows every rating, but it can't say how far away anything is.

ProPersonalised drive times and the drive-time filter are available with Pro. Local sunrise and sunset times for your home work on Lite too.
