Guide/Getting started

Your first
five minutes.

A quick route through PhotoCast, from setting your home to deciding whether tomorrow's sunrise is worth the alarm. Each step links to the full guide if you want more.

1 · Set your home

If the top of the screen says Set a postcode, tap it and enter your postcode. It takes a few seconds and gives you the local sunrise and sunset times, with blue hour and golden hour, for where you live. (Blue hour is the deep-blue twilight just before sunrise and just after sunset; golden hour is the warm, low-angled light around them.)

On Pro it also works out the drive time to every location, so PhotoCast can show what's within reach.

More on your home location

2 · Scan the verdicts

PhotoCast opens on At a Glance. Each card is one sunrise or sunset over the next few days, with a short verdict:

Worth it
Conditions look promising at some locations.
Maybe
Mixed, but worth a look.
Poor
Probably one to skip.

On Pro you can limit "Worth it" to locations within a drive time you choose.

Look out for the small labels under the dates too. They highlight notable conditions or events, such as a spring tide or a cloud inversion. A good verdict and a notable condition on the same morning is often the combination worth driving for.

More on At a Glance

3 · Explore a promising forecast

Tap a Worth it or Maybe card. You'll see a larger map in the forecast colours, the regions ranked from best to worst, and the individual locations with their star ratings. Each star rating runs from one to five: one or two means the light is unlikely to reward a trip, three means some colour is possible but not reliable, four means conditions look good, and five is kept for the rare occasions when everything looks set to come together.

On Pro, a single Best bet across the forecast names the sunrise or sunset and the region that look strongest, with a short reason.

More on opening a sunrise or sunset

4 · Check a location

Tap a location for its own forecast: its rating for each sunrise and sunset, and the times of blue hour and golden hour. If you have a favourite place in mind, the Map is the quickest way to find it.

On the coast, check the tide too. The tide can change a coastal composition completely. Check whether the water level will suit the photograph you have in mind at sunrise or sunset. The Tide panel on the Map shows where the water will be at that moment.

ProFiery Sky and Golden Hour scores, drive times, leave-by times and Tide at the light are available with Pro.

5 · Decide, then check again

Forecasts are refreshed three times a day, and they're more reliable closer to the event. If tomorrow looks good tonight, look again in the morning before you set off. A provisional rating means confidence is lower, so it is especially worth checking again before you set off.
