Guide/Getting started

Your first
five minutes.

A quick route through PhotoCast, from setting your home to deciding whether tomorrow's sunrise is worth the alarm. Each step links to the full guide if you want more.

1 · Set your home

If the top of the screen says Set a postcode, tap it and enter your postcode. It takes a few seconds and gives you sunrise and sunset times for where you live.

On Pro it also works out the drive to every location, so PhotoCast can show what's within reach.

More on your home location

2 · Scan the verdicts

PhotoCast opens on At a Glance. Each card is one sunrise or sunset over the next few days, with a one-word verdict:

Worth it
Conditions look promising somewhere you could reach.
Maybe
Mixed, but worth a look.
Poor
Probably one to skip.

Look out for topic pills under the dates too, such as a spring tide or a cloud inversion. A good verdict and a topic on the same morning is often the combination worth driving for.

More on At a Glance

3 · Open the promising one

Tap a Worth it or Maybe card. You'll see a larger map in the forecast colours, the regions ranked from best to worst, and the individual locations with their star ratings.

On Pro, the Best bet names the region that looks strongest and says why.

More on opening a sunrise or sunset

4 · Check a location

Tap a location for its own forecast: its rating for each sunrise and sunset, and the times of blue hour and golden hour. If you have a favourite place in mind, the Map is the quickest way to find it.

On the coast, check the tide too. A strong sunrise at the wrong state of tide is the commonest coastal disappointment, and the Tide panel on the Map shows where the water will be at the moment of the light.

ProFiery Sky and Golden Hour scores, drive and leave-by times and the tide at the light are available with Pro.

5 · Decide, then check again

Forecasts are refreshed three times a day, and they're more reliable closer to the event. If tomorrow looks good tonight, look again in the morning before you set off. A rating marked provisional is the one most likely to change.
