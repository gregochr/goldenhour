Guide/Getting started

Your first
five minutes.

PhotoCast is one screen, and a shoot is planned on it in four steps: when to go, which morning looks best, where to stand, and the details you need to go. Each step links to the full guide if you want more.

Before you start: set your home

If the top of the screen says Set a postcode, tap it and enter your postcode. It takes a few seconds and gives you the local sunrise and sunset times, with blue hour and golden hour, for where you live: today's on a computer's top line, and each sunrise's or sunset's in the map's header. (Blue hour is the deep-blue twilight just before sunrise and just after sunset; golden hour is the warm, low-angled light around them.)

On Pro it also works out the drive time to every location, so PhotoCast can show what's within reach. Settings, and the guide, are in the ☰ menu at the top right.

More on your home location

1 · When: Further ahead

Start with the calendar. Scroll down, or press Further ahead ↓ in the This week heading, to see the dates in the next 90 days that are known in advance: big tides, supermoons, meteor showers and eclipses. If a date falls on a day you are free, you have found your when.

ProPress Watch this date on an entry and it moves into Watching at the top of Further ahead. When the date comes into the forecast, it becomes a lane on its day in This week with the plan for that morning.

Most days you won't need to look this far ahead, and can go straight to the next step.

More on Further ahead and watching a date

2 · Which morning: This week

This week is the top of the screen. Each card is one sunrise or sunset over the next few days, with a short verdict:

Worth it
Conditions look promising at some locations.
Maybe
Mixed, but worth a look.
Poor
Probably one to skip.

On Pro you can limit "Worth it" to locations within a drive time you choose.

Look out for the event lanes under the dates too: one row for each event, stretching across the days it covers. They highlight notable conditions or events, such as a spring tide or a cloud inversion. A good verdict and a notable condition on the same morning is often the combination worth driving for.

On Pro, a single Best bet across the forecast names the sunrise or sunset and the region that look strongest, with a short reason.

More on This week

3 · Where: the map

Tap a Worth it or Maybe card. The map opens over the screen, on that sunrise or sunset, framed (when you have a home set) to show your home and the best place together, with that place's callout already open. A lane or a Your places chip opens it the same way. From a card, its little map picture grows into the map as it opens. You'll see the forecast colours across the region, the regions ranked from best to worst, and the individual locations with their star ratings. Each star rating runs from one to five: one or two means the light is unlikely to reward a trip, three means some colour is possible but not reliable, four means conditions look good, and five is kept for the rare occasions when everything looks set to come together.

Use ‹ and › in the map's header to step to the sunrise or sunset before or after. When you have finished, ✕ or your browser's Back button closes the map and returns you to the card you came from.

More on the map

4 · Go: the callout and Leave by

Tap a location on the map for its callout: its rating for that sunrise or sunset, and how far away it is. If you already know where you want to go, ⌕ Find in the map's header is the quickest way to reach it. For a location's own forecast over the coming days, open it from the callout, with the times of blue hour and golden hour.

On the coast, check the tide too. The tide can change a coastal composition completely, so check whether the water level will suit the photograph you have in mind at sunrise or sunset. The Tide panel on the map shows where the water will be at that moment.

ProRed Sky and Golden Hour scores, drive times, a Leave by time worked out from your drive time and Tide at the light are available with Pro.

Decide, then check again

Forecasts are refreshed three times a day, and they're more reliable closer to the event. If tomorrow looks good tonight, look again in the morning before you set off. A provisional rating means confidence is lower, so it is especially worth checking again before you set off.
