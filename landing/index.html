02 · What PhotoCast does

A forecast for the shot, not just the weather.

Good photographic light depends on more than whether it's sunny or cloudy.

/ 01

See where the light looks promising

Explore sunrise and sunset forecasts for more than 200 locations across northern England.

The colour-coded map and star ratings make it easy to compare conditions, whether you have a particular location in mind or you're looking for somewhere new.

/ 02

Understand what makes the light

Cloud isn't always a bad thing. In fact, the right cloud in the right place can make all the difference to a sunrise or sunset.

PhotoCast examines cloud layers, humidity, airborne particles and the direction of the light to assess the potential for colourful skies and golden-hour conditions.

Simple ratings give you the overview. With Pro, you can explore two separate assessments:

Fiery Sky looks at the potential for colourful skies around sunrise and sunset.

Golden Hour considers the conditions for warm, directional light that can bring shape and depth to the landscape.

You'll also find explanations of the atmospheric conditions behind each forecast.

/ 03

Find your next opportunity

Not sure where to go this weekend?

Compare promising locations and times across the region over the next few days. With Pro, best-bet recommendations help narrow down the choice.

/ 04

Ask PhotoCast Pro

Sometimes a rating only tells half the story.

Why does a location look promising? What's happening with the cloud layers? Could the conditions favour a colourful sunrise or softer golden-hour light?

Ask PhotoCast lets you explore the forecast in your own words, with straightforward explanations based on the available weather information.

Whether you're curious about the science or simply deciding where to go, it's a useful way to understand what's behind the forecast.

/ 05

Plan around the tides

For coastal photographers, the tide can be just as important as the light.

PhotoCast brings tidal information together with sunrise and sunset forecasts, helping you spot interesting combinations of water levels and photographic light.

Aurora sightings are never guaranteed, but knowing when conditions are favourable can make all the difference.

Some of the most atmospheric landscape photographs happen when mist settles into valleys or low cloud fills the landscape beneath the hills.

04 · The story

It started with one question about one sunrise.

One evening last spring, I found myself wondering whether the following morning's sunrise would be worth getting up for.

I asked Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, what the conditions looked like. It thought they were promising. That got me curious about what actually makes a good sunrise — not just the amount of cloud, but its height and position, the moisture in the air, and the particles that scatter the light.

I started looking for a tool that brought all those things together for photographers. There were plenty of weather forecasts, of course, and even some focussed on photography, but nothing quite like what I had in mind.

In the end, I decided against the early start. But the question stayed with me.

I've spent 25 years building software systems for major financial institutions, so I began exploring whether I could build something myself.

That experiment became PhotoCast.

Today, it brings together more than ten atmospheric variables to assess sunrise and sunset conditions across over 200 locations.

I wanted a little more confidence when deciding whether to set that early alarm.

06 · A few common questions

A little more about PhotoCast.

How accurate are the forecasts?

Weather and light are never completely predictable, so PhotoCast can't guarantee a spectacular sunrise or sunset.

It uses weather forecasts and atmospheric information to help identify promising conditions. Forecasts are generally more useful closer to the event, particularly for the following day.

Think of it as another tool to help you make an informed decision, rather than a promise of what the sky will do.

Do I need to understand weather charts?

Not at all. The map and star ratings give you a simple overview, even if you're new to landscape photography.

Where does PhotoCast cover?

PhotoCast currently covers more than 200 locations across northern England, including Northumberland, the Lake District, North Yorkshire, the Yorkshire Dales, the North York Moors, Tyne and Wear and Teesdale.

How far ahead can I plan?

PhotoCast forecasts sunrise and sunset conditions for the next few days. Forecasts are generally most reliable for the following morning or evening.

How often are forecasts updated?

Three times a day. It's worth checking again the evening before, or shortly before you set off, as the outlook can change.
