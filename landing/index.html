02 · What it does

A forecast for the shot, not just the weather.

Good light depends on more than whether the forecast says sunny or cloudy. PhotoCast brings together the atmospheric conditions that can make a difference to your photographs, helping you decide when and where to head out.

Look beyond the cloud forecast

Not all cloudy skies are the same. The height and thickness of cloud layers, atmospheric moisture and particles in the air can all influence the colours and quality of the light.

PhotoCast brings these factors together to assess the potential for colourful skies and golden-hour light, with separate scores and straightforward explanations to help you understand the forecast.

Some of the most memorable landscape photographs happen when mist settles into valleys or low cloud fills the landscape beneath the hills.

It's another way to spot opportunities that might not be obvious from a standard weather forecast.

04 · The story

It started with one question about one sunrise.

One evening, I found myself wondering whether the following morning's sunrise would be worth the early alarm and the drive.

Like many landscape photographers, I'd spent plenty of time checking weather forecasts, looking at cloud cover and hoping the conditions would come together. Sometimes they did. Sometimes they didn't.

I started looking more closely at what makes a promising sunrise: the different layers of cloud, moisture in the atmosphere, particles that scatter the light, and the direction of the rising sun.

I wondered whether all that information could be brought together into something genuinely useful for photographers. Not a guarantee of a beautiful sunrise, but a better-informed idea of what might happen, and where.

That was the beginning of PhotoCast.

Using AI alongside detailed weather and atmospheric data, I began developing a forecasting tool that could assess conditions across hundreds of locations and explain its reasoning in everyday language.

My background is in software engineering, with 25 years spent building reliable systems for major financial institutions. PhotoCast is a rather different sort of project, but I've brought the same care and attention to how it works.

It's still growing, and there's plenty more I want to add. But the aim remains the same: to help photographers spend less time piecing together forecasts and more time making the most of the light.

06 · Common questions

Questions we hear at 4am.

How accurate is PhotoCast?

Weather and light are never completely predictable, so PhotoCast can't guarantee a spectacular sunrise or sunset.

It uses weather forecasts, atmospheric data and AI analysis to help identify promising conditions. Forecasts are generally more useful closer to the event, particularly for the following day.

Think of PhotoCast as another tool to help you make an informed decision, rather than a promise of what the sky will do.

Where can I use PhotoCast?

PhotoCast currently covers more than 200 locations across northern England, including Northumberland, the Lake District, North Yorkshire, the Yorkshire Dales, the North York Moors, Tyne and Wear and Teesdale.

Locations include coastal viewpoints, lakes, mountains, valleys and other popular landscape photography spots.

What's the difference between Lite and Pro?

Lite gives you a free three-day overview of sunrise and sunset conditions, with location ratings, a forecast map and a planning grid.

Pro is designed for photographers who'd like to explore the detail behind those forecasts. It adds more in-depth light analysis, written explanations, recommended locations, aurora forecasts and additional planning tools.

You can start with Lite and decide later whether Pro would be useful to you.

Do I need to understand weather charts to use PhotoCast?

Not at all. PhotoCast is designed to give you a straightforward overview of the conditions, even if you're new to landscape photography.

If you're more experienced, you can explore the detailed forecasts and atmospheric information behind the ratings.

Use as much or as little detail as you find helpful.
