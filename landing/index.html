02 · What PhotoCast does

A forecast for the shot, not just the weather.

Good photographic light depends on more than whether it's sunny or cloudy.

/ 01

See where the light looks good

Explore sunrise and sunset forecasts for more than 200 locations across northern England.

PhotoCast's colour-coded map and star ratings make it easy to compare conditions, whether you have a particular location in mind or you're looking for somewhere new.

/ 02

Understand what makes the light

Cloud isn't always a bad thing. The right cloud in the right place can make all the difference to a sunrise or sunset.

PhotoCast examines cloud layers, humidity, airborne particles and the direction of the light. With PhotoCast Pro you get two separate scores, each with an explanation: Fiery Sky for colourful skies, and Golden Hour for warm, directional light that brings shape and depth to the landscape.

/ 03

Find your next opportunity

Not sure where to go this weekend?

Compare locations and times worth considering across the region over the next few days. With Pro, best-bet recommendations help narrow down the choice.

/ 04

Ask PhotoCast Pro

Sometimes a rating only tells half the story.

Why does a location look promising? What are the cloud layers doing? Is it a fiery-sunrise morning or a softer golden-hour one? Ask PhotoCast in your own words and get a straightforward answer from the forecast.

/ 05

Plan around the tides

For coastal photographers, the tide can be just as important as the light.

PhotoCast takes tidal conditions into account when rating coastal locations, showing the state of the tide at sunrise and sunset and highlighting notable events such as spring tides.

With Pro, PhotoCast goes further. It identifies when the preferred tide for each coastal location aligns with sunrise or sunset, helping you find the best opportunities for combining the right water level with beautiful light.

Coastal safety information →

/ 06

Keep an eye on the northern lights Pro

Aurora sightings are never guaranteed, but knowing when conditions are favourable can make all the difference.

PhotoCast uses space weather information to help identify nights when the northern lights may be worth looking out for, giving you time to consider where to go.

/ 07

Spot promising mist and cloud inversions

Some of the most atmospheric landscape photographs happen when mist settles in valleys or low cloud gathers below the hills.

PhotoCast looks for the conditions that help mist and low cloud form, checks whether you could photograph it from above or within it, and considers overnight conditions and winds on higher ground—so you can see which mornings are worth an early start.

04 · The story

It started with one question about one sunrise.

One evening last spring, I found myself wondering whether the following morning's sunrise would be worth getting up for.

I asked Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, what the conditions looked like. It thought they were promising. That got me curious about what actually makes a good sunrise — not just the amount of cloud, but its height and position, the moisture in the air, and the particles that scatter the light.

I started looking for a tool that brought all those things together for photographers. There were plenty of weather forecasts, of course, and even some focused on photography, but nothing quite like what I had in mind.

In the end, I decided against the early start. But the question stayed with me.

I've spent 25 years building software systems for major financial institutions, so I began exploring whether I could build something myself.

That experiment became PhotoCast.

Today, it brings together more than ten atmospheric variables to assess sunrise and sunset conditions across over 200 locations.

I wanted a little more confidence when deciding whether to set that early alarm.

That's still what PhotoCast is about. It won't always get the weather right, but hopefully it can help other photographers make the most of the opportunities that come along.

06 · A few common questions

A little more about PhotoCast.

How accurate are the forecasts?

PhotoCast uses weather and atmospheric information to identify promising conditions for photographic light.

Forecasts are generally more reliable closer to the event, particularly for the following morning or evening. But the weather can always surprise us, so the ratings are a guide rather than a guarantee.

Do I need to understand weather charts?

Not at all. The map and star ratings give you a simple overview, even if you're new to landscape photography.

Which areas does PhotoCast cover?

PhotoCast currently covers more than 200 locations across northern England, including Northumberland, the Lake District, North Yorkshire, the Yorkshire Dales, the North York Moors, Tyne and Wear and Teesdale.

How far ahead can I see forecasts?

PhotoCast forecasts sunrise and sunset conditions for the next few days. Forecasts are generally most reliable for the following morning or evening.

How often are forecasts updated?

Three times a day. It's worth checking again the evening before, or shortly before you set off, as the outlook can change.
